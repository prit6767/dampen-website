Dampen listens through your computer or phone mic, estimates the sound around you, and sends an anti-noise style signal into your headphones. It is an experimental browser version of noise cancellation for studying, focus, and everyday use.

This version is headphone-focused: it uses the device mic for input and sends processed anti-noise plus a coverage bed into the headphone output. Steady sounds like fans and AC will work better than sudden speech.

Room input

What the computer or phone microphone hears around you.

Anti-noise output

What Dampen is sending into your headphones as a cancellation and coverage layer.

How it behaves

  • Uses the device mic when your headset has no mic or you do not want to use it.
  • Builds an adaptive room profile, then outputs an inverted filtered layer plus masking energy for steady ambient sounds.
  • Keeps retraining the room model during quieter moments instead of asking users to manage settings.

Best result

  • Use closed-back headphones, earbuds, or earphones. Do not use open speakers.
  • Give it one or two seconds of room tone after starting so it can lock onto the space.
  • Use higher strength mostly for fans, AC, road noise, and hum. Sudden voices are harder to cancel.
Signal path: device microphone, room analysis, adaptive anti-noise synthesis, and headphone output. Everything runs locally on-device.
